About

From roughly 1950 onwards, mathematical logic in Prague has begun to flourish. This project investigates the significance and context of the works of three Czech logicians, Ladislav Svante Rieger, Petr Vopěnka, and Petr Hájek, each of whom played a key part in this process and initiated an ongoing tradition.

Reasoning from first principles became a hallmark of the Prague school of formal logic. Working axiomatically enabled deliberate departures from established practices towards entirely new ways of doing and thinking about mathematics. Key topics of interest in the project span various set-theoretic foundations of mathematics, competing views, and pluralism. The work of the three logicians can be fruitfully investigated against the backdrop of finitism. Vopěnka also claimed an affinity with the phenomenological tradition stemming from Husserl's work. Additionally, the early interest in computing, cybernetics, and artificial intelligence branched out into interdisciplinary pursuits.

These creative endeavours do not stand in isolation, but inherit the legacy of Leibniz, Bolzano, Cantor, Husserl, Hilbert, Skolem, Carnap, and many others. They developed alongside other local enterprises, including the pioneering work of Otakar Zich and the phenomenological school of Jan Patočka. The tradition has also informed subsequent research in arithmetic and nonclassical logic. The project advances this wealth of ideas by making it available in archives of formalized mathematical results.

Timeline

  • January 2025. Project started
  • August 2025. The New Scientist published a feature on ultrafinitism based on the Ultrafinitism Conference, referring to our work on Vopěnka.
  • October 2025. We co-organized Horizons: Conference in Honour of Petr Vopěnka at the AKC in Prague.
  • November 2025. Aleksi Honkasalo joined the project.
  • December 2025. We retrieved an early unpublished manuscript about the Alternative Set Theory from Vopěnka archive at Department of Philosophy, University of West Bohemia in Pilsen.
  • April 2026. Jonathan Osinski joined the project.
  • November 2026. Roman Kossak's research visit to ICS CAS.
